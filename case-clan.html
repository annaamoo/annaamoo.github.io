CASE STUDY · FINTECH · BNPL · ZERO TO ONE

Building a BNPL platform that helped customers buy and merchants grow.

At Clan, I helped take a two-sided commerce and payments product from market research through launch. We set out to address a straightforward problem: customers could not always afford to pay in full upfront, while merchants needed more ways to close sales without waiting for installment repayments.

THE PRODUCT THESIS

Unlock consumer purchases without making merchants wait for installments.

Consumers could receive products and repay on automated schedules. Merchants showcased products through dedicated e-commerce dashboards, offered BNPL at checkout and received full payment after successful transactions.

MARKET OPPORTUNITY

One affordability barrier, two customer problems.

CONSUMERS

The upfront payment was the obstacle.

Customers wanted products but could not always pay the full purchase price immediately. Installments offered a way to purchase now and manage repayment over time.

MERCHANTS

More payment choice could unlock sales.

Merchants needed to showcase their inventory and accept flexible payment methods without waiting through the customer's repayment period to receive their proceeds.

I conducted market research and helped shape the offering around value for both parties, not simply an alternative checkout button.

DECISIONS AND OWNERSHIP

The hard part was balancing access, risk and reliable payments.

01

Research before requirements

I researched the market, identified the affordability opportunity, and helped define the product's consumer and merchant propositions.

02

Shape eligibility criteria

I developed eligibility and psychometric assessment questions, identified information to evaluate and reviewed the approach with the team.

03

Test the actual logic

I used Postman to test the eligibility system, API behavior, calculations and decision outcomes—not just the application interface.

04

Make repayments fit real income

I worked on flexibility in automated repayment schedules so installment timing better reflected customers' earning patterns.

The central trade-off

Improving access to purchases had to be balanced with eligibility requirements, payment processing, risk, compliance and repayment obligations. I collaborated with engineering, finance, risk, compliance and payment providers to shape workable experiences.
